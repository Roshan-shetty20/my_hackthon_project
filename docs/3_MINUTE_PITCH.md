# Crisis Command: 3-Minute Pitch

## 0:00–0:20 | The Problem
In an urban emergency, disaster doesn't happen one event at a time. It cascades. A chemical spill happens while an ambulance breaks down and a major road floods. 
When command centers rely on static dashboards, human dispatchers become cognitively overwhelmed trying to recalculate global logistics in their heads. When they try to rely purely on generative AI, they risk deadly hallucinations.

## 0:20–0:45 | What Crisis Command Is
Crisis Command is a resilient, explainable multi-agent emergency-response decision-support copilot. 
It bridges the gap between rigid dashboards and unpredictable AI by isolating the math from the language. 

## 0:45–1:15 | The Planning Engine
When multiple incidents strike simultaneously, our deterministic planning engine calculates true resource demand, enforces hard constraints, and generates multiple feasible response plans. 
But it doesn’t stop there. It runs those plans through a rigorous counterfactual stress-test against 100 potential future failure scenarios. 

## 1:15–1:45 | Plan A/B/C + Human Approval
The system presents the Human Commander with Plan A, B, and C. The deterministic engine identifies the mathematically most resilient plan. Then, Google Gemini acts purely as an explanation layer—reading the math and summarizing the strategic tradeoffs, risks, and Single Points of Failure in plain language.
The AI does not dispatch. The human commander reviews the evidence and retains absolute operational authority.

## 1:45–2:15 | Disruption & Replanning
Emergencies are highly volatile. What happens when a route floods or a dispatched unit fails? 
Crisis Command recalculates the global impact instantly. 

## 2:15–2:40 | Simultaneous Crisis + Global Impact
When cascading failures hit simultaneously—a unit breakdown coupled with a new critical incident—the system ingests them atomically. It generates a new global strategy and produces a surgical "Plan Diff" so the commander knows exactly what changed, who is being reassigned, and why.

## 2:40–3:00 | The Differentiator
Crisis Command is designed to reduce avoidable response delays and preserve critical incident coverage.
Don't just find a response plan. Find a response plan that can survive the next surprise.
